# Source verification

## Choose authority by the proposition

- Use legislation and regulations for legal requirements, including amendments, commencement, transitional provisions, and historical versions.
- Use the actual instrument for ministerial instructions and public policies. Check scope, authority, conditions, start/end dates, and whether it remains operative.
- Use IRCC Program Delivery Instructions for administrative guidance, program pages and Help Centre for applicant-facing instructions, and the responsible province/territory for provincial criteria and intake status.
- Read judgments for judicial reasoning. A judgment reproduced by a reliable legal publisher remains primary text; a headnote, news story, or practitioner's summary does not.
- Use commentary and field notes as research leads or clearly labelled observations. Do not conceal their provenance or claim access to internal government material.

Treat authority as issue-specific: judicial interpretation and program guidance do not fit a simple “newest page wins” rule. Distinguish legal force, scope, and relevant facts.

## Research efficiently

1. Frame the issue using program, legal requirement, key event date, and any exception.
2. Search the responsible official domain. Open the relevant provisions and guidance rather than just a directory or results page.
3. Verify decisive exceptions, recent changes, and transition provisions. Open linked details when needed.
4. Record the source title, publisher, URL, supported proposition, effective/version date, and access date. Use this evidence record for the answer; do not overwhelm the user with retrieval logs.
5. Resolve conflicting versions. If a conflict cannot be resolved, explain its practical effect and narrow the conclusion.
6. Stop when the material claim and exceptions are adequately supported. Do not pad the answer with redundant sources.

For “latest” questions, state an as-of date based on the actual session date. Do not mistake the date a webpage was accessed or last modified for a rule's effective date. Use the user's timezone for user-facing dates; use the governing filing timezone for deadlines.

If an official judgment host blocks access, seek the same judgment on a reliable primary-text repository and disclose any material verification limit.

## Source failures

- If a URL is broken, search the official title and domain for its replacement. Do not silently substitute a third-party mirror as an official source.
- If only a snippet or commentary is available, describe it as a lead. Do not attribute unseen text or quotations to the original.
- If an uploaded policy is undated, archived, or inconsistent with the requested date, establish applicability before relying on it.
- If browsing/tools are unavailable, explain the limitation once. Analyze supplied material conditionally and identify the minimum authoritative checks still needed.
- Do not claim comprehensive coverage, negative case treatment, or an exhaustive search unless the available evidence supports that statement.
