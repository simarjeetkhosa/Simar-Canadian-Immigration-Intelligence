# NOC and TEER research

## Establish the job and classification version

Use the applicant's actual work, not a preferred immigration outcome. Collect the relevant job description, ordinary duties, work setting, products/services, reporting line, decision-making authority, supervision, dates and any required licensing. Use an anonymized description for external searches.

Verify which NOC version the intended immigration program applies at the relevant date. Start with [IRCC's NOC guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/find-national-occupation-code.html) and [the official NOC database](https://noc.esdc.gc.ca/). A newly announced classification edition is not automatically the edition adopted by IRCC. Do not mix codes from different editions; use official concordance tables where necessary and verify the actual duties again.

## Compare candidates

1. Search titles and duty keywords to find plausible unit groups. Use the official advanced search for lead statements, duties and exclusions.
2. Open the full profiles of the most plausible groups. Read the lead statement, main duties, illustrative titles, exclusions and employment requirements.
3. Compare the applicant's actual responsibilities against the lead statement and duties, paying attention to exclusions and the distinction between managerial, supervisory and hands-on work.
4. Apply the exact work-experience test of the intended immigration program. Do not invent a universal matching percentage or claim that a title match alone satisfies it.
5. Distinguish occupational classification from professional licensing, wage compliance, work authorization, experience eligibility and a program's occupation list. A correct NOC does not settle the other requirements.
6. For multiple roles or promotions, assess each materially different work period separately. Do not automatically put all employment under the highest-level title.

Return a provisional best fit only when duties support it. If several groups remain plausible, explain the decisive distinction and ask focused questions.

Use a duty-by-duty evidence table for a contested or detailed assessment. Cite the actual profiles, not only the NOC homepage. State what evidence would change the recommendation.

## Protect the accuracy of employment evidence

Write employer letters in the employer's ordinary language, using only confirmed duties. Do not copy every official NOC duty into a letter or add managerial/supervisory responsibilities to obtain a preferred code. Do not claim exact matching percentages without a justified method, and never invent a mandatory percentage.

Flag job-title inflation, contradictory hours/dates, duties inconsistent with the business, incompatible NOC editions, unexplained role changes, or unsupported licensing claims as matters to resolve, not automatic findings of fraud. If an employer's draft is inaccurate, propose factually faithful wording and identify what the employer must confirm.
