# Express Entry, provincial and community pathways

## Route before scoring

Separate eligibility for a federal program, entry to a candidate pool, provincial/community selection, invitation, nomination/recommendation, federal application, and final approval. Do not present a nomination, job offer, low historical cut-off, or community recommendation as guaranteed selection or PR.

For Express Entry, verify the applicable Canadian Experience Class, Federal Skilled Worker Program, or Federal Skilled Trades Program criteria, the current CRS rules, and any category-based criteria. Check the exact occupation list, required experience window, language condition, round type and tie-break rule. Distinguish a category from a separate immigration program. Use verified historical draws for context, never as a prediction or a promise.

For non-Express Entry, identify the actual federal or provincial route and its own requirements. Do not apply a CRS cut-off to a base PNP stream. Verify both provincial nomination and federal PR requirements, document validity, nomination conditions, settlement intention and application deadlines. Assess family sponsorship, Atlantic, caregiver, business, Quebec, humanitarian or other routes only when relevant; do not lump them into one generic non-Express Entry program.

Handle Quebec's selection system and Nunavut separately; do not label them PNP jurisdictions. Confirm any later structural changes through official sources.

For “all PNP streams,” enumerate the streams on each current official index, including worker, graduate, entrepreneur/business and pilot routes where listed. Record renamed, paused, closed and invitation-only streams separately. Verify stream criteria, notices, intake windows, EOI/NOI/invitation process, allocations and any current draw information. A province's landing page alone does not verify every stream.

Use a coverage ledger. If research is incomplete, mark that jurisdiction or stream “not yet verified” and identify the gap; never call a partial scan comprehensive. For a single client's assessment, give a concise shortlist first and summarize why the other jurisdictions were screened out when an all-province scan was requested.

Do not substitute economic admissions targets for nomination allocations, available invitations, or a particular client's opportunity. A stream accepting EOIs may not be accepting applications from everyone.

## AAIP deep review

Open [AAIP's stream index](https://www.alberta.ca/aaip-application-streams), each relevant stream page, selection notices and employer requirements. Build the current inventory rather than freezing an eligibility list in this skill.

Use these names as discovery prompts, not proof of availability: Alberta Opportunity Stream, Alberta Express Entry Stream and dedicated pathways, Tourism and Hospitality Stream, Rural Renewal Stream, and entrepreneur/farm streams. Distinguish a stream from a pathway within it.

Check the worker EOI and invitation process separately from minimum eligibility. Verify occupation exclusions, permit/status eligibility, Alberta employment, employer and job-offer requirements, language/ECA, community designation/endorsement where applicable, sector restrictions, current allocations and processing information. For a health, technology or other dedicated pathway, verify the precise route and any professional licensing or employer requirements.

Never confuse AAIP Rural Renewal with the federal RCIP. Do not infer eligibility from living in a rural area, having an Alberta offer, or meeting a score threshold alone.

## RCIP, FCIP and Atlantic routes

For RCIP, start at [IRCC's Rural Community Immigration Pilot](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/rural-franco-pilots/rural-immigration.html). Follow official links to participating communities and their authorized program bodies. Treat federal, community and employer requirements as separate checks.

Verify the actual community boundary, current priority sectors/occupations, designated employer, available intake, recommendation requirements, job-offer conditions and the applicant's experience, education, language, funds and settlement intention. Record any graduate exemption only after verifying its conditions. A designated employer list does not establish a vacancy, a valid offer, or a recommendation.

Research FCIP separately under its own language and community conditions. Research the Atlantic Immigration Program through IRCC and the responsible Atlantic province; distinguish employer designation, endorsement, settlement support and federal eligibility. Do not treat these federal/community pathways as interchangeable with PNP.

Check optional work-permit eligibility and family eligibility under separate current instructions. Do not assume PR eligibility, an employer designation, or a recommendation automatically authorizes work.
