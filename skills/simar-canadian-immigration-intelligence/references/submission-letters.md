# Natural, evidence-based submission letters

## Identify the voice and purpose

Draft clear, specific professional prose that reads naturally. “Human-written” means a natural writing style here; do not falsely claim a human authored, reviewed or signed the text.

Identify the application/response type, requested decision, recipient, writer's role, material facts, issues and available evidence. Use first person for an applicant's own explanation only when requested and supported by their account. Use a representative voice only when that role is established. Do not invent representation, credentials, an authorization form or a signature.

Use this workflow for initial submissions, letters of explanation, work/study/visitor applications, PR/PNP/community applications, procedural fairness responses and reconsideration. For refusals or deadlines, also read the refusal workflow before drafting.

## Build the evidence before the prose

1. Make a short chronology and a private fact-to-document ledger. Identify contradictions between forms, earlier applications, letters, dates, duties, financial records and the current explanation.
2. Verify the current legal/program requirements at the relevant date and map each material issue to evidence.
3. Decide what the letter needs to establish. Organize around the actual decision criteria or officer's stated concerns, not a generic immigration essay.
4. Ask only for essential missing facts. Draft the supported portions now; use clear placeholders where needed. Never fill a gap with a persuasive invention.
5. Include only the authorities that help resolve a real issue. Avoid turning a straightforward factual cover letter into an unnecessary case-law brief.

## Write naturally

- Begin with the application and specific request. Explain the strongest relevant facts in a logical sequence.
- Use concrete dates, roles, amounts and document references where verified. Explain why each fact matters.
- Vary sentence length naturally. Prefer direct verbs and ordinary professional language.
- Avoid repetitive “Furthermore/Moreover/In conclusion,” inflated praise of the applicant, emotional embellishment, generic declarations of compliance and “I hope this letter finds you well” in a submission.
- Avoid em dashes and en dashes in drafted letters; use punctuation and full sentences. Keep official names, legal terms and quotations accurate, while explaining them plainly outside quotations.
- Preserve uncertainty and conditional claims. Do not turn “may meet” into “meets” or a hope into a guarantee.
- Describe adverse facts honestly and respond with supported explanations. Do not omit a material prior refusal, status problem, work issue or document inconsistency to make the letter stronger.
- Use headings only where they improve a substantial letter. Avoid excessive bolding, long checklists inside prose, repetitive summaries and copied NOC wording.
- Match the requested language. Preserve the same substance in Punjabi and English versions; do not call a translation certified without that service.

## Suggested structure

Use only the parts needed:

1. Date, recipient and accurate subject/application reference.
2. Purpose and requested decision.
3. Relevant facts and chronology.
4. Application of the requirements or response to each concern, tied to evidence.
5. Any material qualification or explanation.
6. Specific, appropriate request and supplied sign-off.
7. Enclosure/exhibit list, if documents are actually available.

Keep internal review notes outside the copy-ready letter. Identify unresolved placeholders, factual contradictions, verification limits and missing evidence in a separate short note. Never call the draft filing-ready while material placeholders remain. For a standalone DOCX/PDF request, use the relevant artifact skill and visually verify the finished document.

## Final editorial and evidence pass

Read the letter as a reviewer unfamiliar with the file. Remove stock phrases and repetition. Check every material fact against the ledger, every legal proposition against a verified source, every exhibit against the supplied documents and every name/date against the record. Check that the closing requests relief available in the selected process.

Do not submit, send or sign the letter merely because drafting was requested. Follow the user's actual authorization for any subsequent action.
