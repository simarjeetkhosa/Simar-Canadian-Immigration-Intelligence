# Judicial-review case audit

Treat a JR audit as a structured legal-research and record-review aid for the applicant and appropriate counsel. Do not equate a refusal review with a filed proceeding, a merits appeal or a prediction of success.

## Triage the proceeding and time limits first

Identify the exact decision or omission, tribunal/decision maker, governing statute, application category, decision date, notification/awareness date, procedural stage, available appeal route, prior proceedings, reconsideration requests and any imminent removal or status event. Do not infer where a matter arose solely from where the client is now.

Verify jurisdiction, leave requirements, exhaustion/prerequisites, filing/service deadlines, counting rules and any extension issue using the current legislation, rules, practice directions and actual court orders. A specific case order may change procedural dates. Distinguish immigration and citizenship procedures. Never assume a request for GCMS notes, reconsideration or a new application preserves a court deadline or stays removal.

Use these starting points, opened when this workflow was added on 2026-10-28; recheck them for each live case:
- [IRPA section 72](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-2.5/section-72.html).
- [Federal Courts Citizenship, Immigration and Refugee Protection Rules](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-93-22/).
- The Federal Court's current practice notices, immigration procedural charts, e-filing requirements and the actual orders in the case. Resolve older guidance against current rules instead of copying an outdated service process.

Flag an approaching or uncertain deadline immediately and recommend prompt review by counsel authorized for the proceeding. Continue the audit. Do not wait for a complete record before identifying urgency or claim that an RCIC credential alone establishes authority to represent someone in Federal Court.

## Build the auditable record

Inventory the refusal/decision, reasons, GCMS notes, application and attachments as submitted, fairness notices/responses, correspondence, proof of notification, procedural documents and court orders. Identify missing, unreadable or disputed material.

Create a dated chronology and a document/page index. Mark each item as part of the original decision-making record, later-created evidence, or status unknown. Do not silently insert new evidence into the historical record. Verify the relevant exceptions and admissibility rules before suggesting that new evidence may be used on review.

Audit consistency across forms and prior representations. Treat missing evidence, an officer's disputed factual finding and an alleged legal error as distinct issues.

## Analyze proposed grounds

For each proposed ground:
1. State the challenged finding or procedural event precisely.
2. Identify the applicable legal test and standard of review from verified sources.
3. Link the concern to exact reasons and record passages.
4. Analyze whether it is a reviewable error, a fairness issue, a jurisdictional issue, or disagreement with the outcome without an established reviewable basis.
5. Identify the strongest contrary explanation, adverse authority and factual gaps.
6. Explain materiality and the relief potentially available, without promising approval or redetermination.

Consider issues such as the treatment of central evidence, statutory requirements, reasoning, procedural opportunities or credibility only where the record supports them. For delay/mandamus, identify the duty, process history, explanations and applicable legal test separately. For a removal stay, apply its own current requirements; do not treat filing JR as a stay.

## Produce the audit

For a detailed audit, provide:
- Scope, sources checked and immediate deadline/status concerns.
- Chronology and record completeness.
- A grounds matrix.
- Verified authorities linked to each ground.
- Counterarguments, missing evidence and questions for counsel.
- A practical next-action list and appropriate review status.

Use “supported for counsel review,” “requires further evidence/research,” or “not established on supplied material.” Avoid numerical success probabilities, invented merits scores or unsupported “strong JR” labels.

An audit does not itself initiate, perfect, serve or file a proceeding. Draft requested documents only from the supplied record and verified law. Distinguish an applicant's factual affidavit from legal submissions. Leave oath, signature, certification and final legal approval to the appropriate human process.
